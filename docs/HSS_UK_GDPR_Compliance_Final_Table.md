# HSS UK GDPR Compliance Mapping - Final Table

Last updated: 2026-06-04

This table maps UK GDPR, DPA 2018, PECR, ICO guidance, and HSS safeguarding context into system requirements and implementation recommendations for the HSS Membership Management System.

| S.No | Compliance Area | Article / Regulation | HSS Context | Detailed System Requirement | Recommendations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Lawfulness, Fairness & Transparency | UK GDPR Art. 5(1)(a) | Member registration, parent registration, event registration | Privacy notice must be displayed before registration submission. Record acknowledgement with timestamp, policy version, source, and IP. Do not treat privacy notice acknowledgement as consent. | Authentication & Registration Module: add privacy notice section. Database: store privacy notice version, acknowledgement date, source, and IP. FRD: replace "Privacy Policy Acceptance = Consent" wording. |
| 2 | Lawful Basis for Processing | UK GDPR Art. 6 | Membership, events, safeguarding, donations, communications | Maintain lawful basis per processing activity. Different processing purposes may have different lawful bases. | Super Admin > GDPR Settings: create Processing Activity Register. System Configuration: map membership, events, donations, safeguarding, marketing, and photos/videos to lawful basis. |
| 3 | Child Consent | UK GDPR Art. 8 | Under-13 online account/registration where consent is relied upon | Where consent is relied upon for online service processing, parental responsibility approval is required before activation for children under 13. | Registration Module: under-13 registration requires parent/guardian approval before activation where consent is the lawful basis. Workflow Engine: record parental responsibility approval evidence and verification method. |
| 4 | Child Protection & Safeguarding | UK GDPR child protection expectations + HSS Safeguarding Policy | Age 13-17 members | Apply enhanced child protection. Guardian workflows are HSS safeguarding/operational requirements, not solely GDPR requirements. | Members Module: guardian approval workflow for sensitive profile changes where required by HSS policy. Events Module: parent/guardian approval for selected child events. FRD: label this as a safeguarding requirement. |
| 5 | Special Category Data Protection | UK GDPR Art. 9 | Medical conditions, health data, ethnicity, native language, safeguarding-sensitive data | Special category data requires an Article 6 lawful basis plus a separate Article 9 condition. Access must be restricted and auditable. | Database: encrypt medical/health fields and other high-risk sensitive fields. RBAC: add "View Medical Information" and "View Safeguarding Information" permissions. GDPR Settings: record Article 9 condition. Audit Logs: track every access. |
| 6 | Photos and Videos | UK GDPR Art. 5, 6, 7, 9 where applicable | Member photos, event photos, videos, website/social media usage | Photos/videos are personal data. They are special category data only where they reveal or are processed to infer special category information. | Media Module: separate media consent or legitimate-interest assessment by usage type. Add consent capture for public/social use where needed. Allow opt-out/suppression workflow. |
| 7 | Criminal Offence / DBS Data | UK GDPR Art. 10 + DPA 2018 | DBS verification, safeguarding checks, criminal-record related information | Criminal offence data requires restricted processing, UK-law authorisation, and strict access controls. | Compliance Module: separate DBS permission. GDPR Settings: record DPA 2018 Schedule 1 condition / appropriate policy document where required. Reports Module: exclude DBS data from exports by default. Audit Logs: track every DBS view. |
| 8 | Purpose Limitation | UK GDPR Art. 5(1)(b) | Membership data | Data collected for membership cannot automatically be reused for fundraising, marketing, photos, or external sharing without a valid lawful basis and clear notice. | Communications Module: separate communication preferences. Donations Module: separate lawful basis. Media Module: separate photo/video usage basis. |
| 9 | Data Minimisation | UK GDPR Art. 5(1)(c) | Registration forms, child profiles, event forms | Only collect data necessary for the stated membership, safeguarding, event, donation, or operational purpose. | Registration FRD Review: add business justification against every field. Remove unnecessary fields. Mark optional fields clearly. |
| 10 | Accuracy | UK GDPR Art. 5(1)(d) | Member profiles, guardian details, emergency contacts | Members, guardians, and admins can correct inaccurate information. Maintain change history. | Member Profile Module: change history tab. Audit Logs: record field changed, actor, timestamp, and reason. Mask or encrypt old/new values for sensitive fields. |
| 11 | Storage Limitation | UK GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) | Ex-members, attendance, events, donations, DBS, safeguarding records | Personal data retained only as long as necessary. Legal, safeguarding, accounting, Gift Aid, and DBS retention may justify longer retention. | Super Admin: retention rules screen approved by HSS. Background Jobs: archive/anonymize/delete after retention period. Add "retain with justification" option. |
| 12 | Accountability | UK GDPR Art. 5(2), 24 | Entire platform | Maintain evidence demonstrating compliance with UK GDPR and HSS policies. | Audit Module: consent logs, notice acknowledgements, exports, approvals, deletions, restrictions, breach decisions, processor register changes, and retention actions. |
| 13 | Right to be Informed | UK GDPR Art. 13, 14 | Registration, donations, events, guardians, media, communications | Users must know why data is collected, legal basis, recipients, retention, rights, and sharing purposes. | Create context-specific privacy notices for registration, guardian consent, donations, events, photos/videos, and communications. Store notice version per interaction. |
| 14 | Right of Access (SAR) | UK GDPR Art. 15 | Member/guardian requests | Generate full member data package where appropriate. Include identity verification, scope, exemptions review, third-party data review, and response tracking. | GDPR Module: SAR request workflow. Export Service: PDF + CSV export. SLA Tracking: one-month response target with extension tracking. |
| 15 | Right to Rectification | UK GDPR Art. 16 | Incorrect member/guardian data | Allow correction of inaccurate or outdated data. | Members Module: correction request workflow. Admin Module: correction approval where needed. Audit Logs: record all changes with masked/encrypted sensitive values. |
| 16 | Right to Erasure | UK GDPR Art. 17 | Membership cancellation, withdrawal, inactive records | Support deletion requests while respecting legal, safeguarding, accounting, Gift Aid, DBS, and legal-claims retention requirements. | Add Delete / Archive / Anonymize / Retain with Justification options. Do not hard-delete attendance, donations, DBS, or safeguarding records automatically. |
| 17 | Right to Restrict Processing | UK GDPR Art. 18 | GDPR disputes, contested accuracy, pending erasure review | Temporarily restrict non-essential processing while a dispute or request is reviewed. | Add "Processing Restricted" status. Exclude from non-essential communications, marketing, bulk exports, and optional reporting unless legally or operationally required. |
| 18 | Data Portability | UK GDPR Art. 20 | User-provided data export | Provide machine-readable export only where Article 20 applies: data provided by the individual, processed by automated means, and lawful basis is consent or contract. | GDPR Module: distinguish SAR Export (Art. 15) from Portability Export (Art. 20). Export formats: CSV/JSON. Exclude derived/admin-only/safeguarding notes unless separately required under SAR. |
| 19 | Right to Object | UK GDPR Art. 21 | Communications, legitimate-interest processing | Users can object to direct marketing and certain legitimate-interest processing. Direct marketing objections must always be honoured. | Preference Centre: opt out of newsletters, fundraising, campaign updates, and promotional communications. GDPR Module: legitimate-interest objection review workflow. |
| 20 | PECR & Marketing Preferences | PECR + UK GDPR | Newsletters, fundraising campaigns, SMS/email/app direct marketing | Electronic direct marketing requires PECR-compliant consent or a valid soft opt-in route. Maintain separate preferences and unsubscribe/opt-out mechanisms. | Communications Module: support explicit consent and, where HSS chooses to rely on it, charitable-purpose soft opt-in with opt-out at collection and every message. Keep separate lists for consent-based marketing and soft-opt-in marketing. |
| 21 | Consent Management | UK GDPR Art. 6, 7, 8 | Optional communications, media use, parent/guardian consent | Consent must be specific, informed, freely given, unambiguous, recorded, and withdrawable. | Consent Service: store consent type, subject, guardian/member, version, source, timestamp, IP, withdrawal history, and evidence. Do not use bundled consent for unrelated purposes. |
| 22 | Consent Withdrawal | UK GDPR Art. 7(3) | Members, guardians, donors, communications | Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent. Withdrawal stops optional processing prospectively but does not erase data required for core, legal, safeguarding, or compliance purposes. | Preference Centre: withdrawal controls. Audit Logs: retain withdrawal evidence. Workflow: update downstream communication/media suppression lists. |
| 23 | Privacy by Design & Default | UK GDPR Art. 25 | Entire platform | Privacy and security must be built into architecture and default settings. | Add GDPR review checkpoints in design, development, UAT, and go-live. Defaults: minimum visibility, optional marketing off by default, child accounts restricted by default, exports disabled unless permissioned. |
| 24 | Security of Processing | UK GDPR Art. 32 | All personal data | Apply appropriate technical and organisational security measures proportionate to risk. | Database/storage encryption at rest, encrypted backups, TLS in transit, strong password hashing, MFA/OTP, session timeout, brute-force protection, least-privilege DB users, and column-level encryption for high-risk PII where appropriate. |
| 25 | Searchable Encryption Design | UK GDPR Art. 25, 32 | Member search, admin search, filters, reports | DB encryption at rest should not affect search/sort/filter. Column-level encryption requires separate safe lookup/index design. | Keep operational filter fields plaintext: status, centre, town, region, role, dates. For encrypted email/phone, use normalized digest/blind index for exact lookup. Avoid normal LIKE/ORDER BY on encrypted fields. |
| 26 | Access Control | UK GDPR Art. 25, 32 | Hierarchy model and RBAC | Enforce role-based and hierarchy-based permissions. Users see only data needed for their role and scope. | RBAC: National -> Region -> Town -> Activity Centre visibility. Add sensitive permissions: view contact details, child/guardian data, medical data, DBS data, SAR export, bulk export, erasure approval. |
| 27 | Authentication Security | UK GDPR Art. 32 | Admin and member accounts | Authentication must reduce unauthorised access risk. | OTP/MFA, password policy, secure password hashing, lockout, rate limiting, session timeout, refresh-token rotation, device/session management, and failed-login monitoring. |
| 28 | Audit Logging | UK GDPR Art. 5(2), 24, 32 | Entire platform | Log critical access and changes while avoiding unnecessary duplication of sensitive data in logs. | Audit Module: approvals, profile edits, role assignments, guardian approvals, exports, deletions, restrictions, refunds, donations, DBS views, medical-data views. Mask/encrypt sensitive log payloads. |
| 29 | Records of Processing Activities (ROPA) | UK GDPR Art. 30 | HSS operations | Maintain controller-level records of processing activities, not just technical activity logs. | GDPR Admin: ROPA register with purpose, lawful basis, data categories, data subjects, recipients, processors, transfers, retention, and security measures. |
| 30 | Data Breach Detection | UK GDPR Art. 32, 33 | Security incidents | Detect unauthorised access, suspicious exports, failed logins, privilege escalation, unusual data access, and data leakage indicators. | Security Monitoring: alerts for high-volume exports, repeated failed login attempts, privilege changes, unusual DBS/medical access, and inactive-account access. |
| 31 | Data Breach Notification | UK GDPR Art. 33, 34 | Personal data breaches | Maintain breach workflow, risk assessment, notification decision, ICO deadline tracking, and individual notification decision. | Incident Module: record all breaches including non-reportable breaches. Track 72-hour ICO deadline where reportable. Generate incident reports and notification logs. |
| 32 | Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) | UK GDPR Art. 35 | Children, safeguarding, special category data, large membership database | DPIA should be completed before go-live and before major changes because the platform processes children's data, special category data, safeguarding data, and large membership records. | GDPR Admin: DPIA template and approval workflow. Required for new modules involving children, profiling, special category data, DBS/safeguarding, bulk export, or AI/automated decisioning. |
| 33 | Automated Decision Protection | UK GDPR Art. 22 | Future AI/automated approvals | Any solely automated decision with legal or similarly significant effect requires safeguards and human review. | Do not use automated membership rejection, safeguarding decisions, or eligibility decisions without human review, explanation, override, and audit trail. |
| 34 | International Transfers | UK GDPR Art. 44-49 | AWS/Azure, Stripe, Twilio, SES, analytics, email/SMS providers | Identify hosting locations and international transfers. Ensure transfer mechanism and processor safeguards are documented. | Processor Register: record country/region, subprocessors, SCC/IDTA or adequacy mechanism where applicable, DPA status, and deletion/return terms. |
| 35 | Processor Management | UK GDPR Art. 28 | Stripe, Twilio, SES, hosting, email, analytics, support tools | Use processors only under written terms and documented instructions. | GDPR Admin: processor register with processor name, purpose, data categories, location, DPA link/status, subprocessors, retention, and termination deletion commitments. |
| 36 | Data Sharing Governance | UK GDPR Art. 5, 6, 13, 14, 28 | Event partners, DBS providers, insurers, auditors, accountants | Sharing must match privacy notice, lawful basis, processor/controller role, and minimum necessary data. | Add sharing-purpose register. For event partner sharing, require admin approval, participant notice, export purpose, recipient, fields shared, and audit trail. |
| 37 | Communication Mirroring for Children | HSS Safeguarding Policy + UK GDPR accountability | Under-18 members | High-priority/safeguarding-relevant communications may need to be mirrored to guardian according to HSS policy. | Notifications Module: guardian copy rules for selected event, emergency, safeguarding, and consent messages. Label as HSS safeguarding rule, not standalone GDPR rule. |
| 38 | Emergency Contact Protection | UK GDPR Art. 25 + HSS Safeguarding Policy | Child profiles and emergency handling | Emergency contact data must be accurate, restricted, and editable only by authorised users/guardians where appropriate. | Member Profile: default emergency contact to guardian for child accounts unless HSS allows exception. Make child non-editable where safeguarding policy requires. Audit changes. |
| 39 | Export Monitoring | UK GDPR Art. 5(2), 24, 32 | Reporting module, SAR, admin exports | Every export involving personal data must be logged, justified, and permissioned. | Reports Module: log user, date/time, purpose, filters, record count, fields exported, sensitive fields included, destination if known. Require elevated permission for bulk/sensitive exports. |
| 40 | Data Migration Governance | UK GDPR Art. 5, 25, 32 | Migration of existing member records | Migration must preserve accuracy, security, provenance, and reconciliation evidence. | Migration Plan: validation reports, reconciliation reports, import audit, source mapping, error handling, rollback plan, encryption/key handling, and post-migration access checks. |
| 41 | Retention Management | UK GDPR Art. 5(1)(e), 17 | Members, attendance, events, donations, safeguarding, DBS | Retention rules must be configurable but controlled, documented, and approved by HSS. | Retention Module: retention schedule by data category. Actions: review, archive, anonymize, delete, or retain with justification. Include legal/safeguarding hold. |
| 42 | Incident Management | UK GDPR Art. 32, 33, 34 | Security incidents and safeguarding incidents | Security incidents and safeguarding incidents must have restricted visibility, audit trails, and clear status workflow. | Incident Module: separate security and safeguarding incident types. Restrict visibility. Add timeline, owner, evidence, decisions, notifications, and closure reason. |
| 43 | GDPR Administration | UK GDPR Art. 24 | Super Admin / Data Protection role | Dedicated GDPR administration functions needed for accountability and repeatable compliance operations. | GDPR Admin Panel: SAR requests, erasure/restriction requests, privacy notice versions, lawful basis register, consent logs, breach register, DPIA register, processor register, retention rules. |
| 44 | Policy Versioning & Evidence | UK GDPR Art. 5(2), 13, 14, 24 | Privacy policy, data protection policy, consent text | System must preserve what notice/consent text was shown at the time of acknowledgement or consent. | CMS/GDPR Settings: version privacy notices, consent wording, terms, and communication preference wording. Store immutable evidence snapshot or version reference. |

## Key Implementation Notes

- Do not present privacy notice acknowledgement as consent.
- Article 8 does not require parental consent for all child data processing. It applies where an Information Society Service is offered directly to a child and consent is the lawful basis.
- For age 13-17 members, guardian workflows should be framed mainly as HSS safeguarding/operational requirements.
- Article 9 and Article 10 need explicit design attention because HSS may process health, ethnicity/language, safeguarding, and DBS/criminal-record related data.
- Full DB/storage encryption at rest is compatible with search, sort, and filters. Column-level encryption needs digest/blind-index helpers for lookup fields.
- SAR exports and portability exports are different. Portability is narrower than SAR.
- PECR marketing rules should support explicit consent and, if HSS chooses to rely on it, the charitable-purpose soft opt-in with opt-out controls.
